Validation summary#

Every step was checked against something independent of it: another lab’s processing of the same markers, a different measurement of the same motion, or a physical measurement. The marker residual alone is not enough: it says how tightly the model was fitted to the markers it saw, not whether the joint angles are right (Joint angles: what the residual cannot show).

Sensitivity analysis

Where the numbers come from: the case study (22 box-lifting trials, OptiTrack, 11 subjects; The case study: box lifting with OptiTrack) and four public datasets, all CC BY 4.0: Vielemeyer et al. 2026 (Vicon, ramps), Moissenet & Schreiber 2019 (Qualisys, gait speeds), Maurice et al. 2019 (AnDyDataset, Qualisys with an Xsens suit), and the AddBiomechanics Dataset (Werling et al. 2024; its Falisse et al. 2017 part). The settings and their sources are on Parameters and their sources.

Known limits#

  • Offsets are not errors by themselves. Against Plug-in-Gait the hip flexion is about 10° lower and the elbow 6–12°; against Xsens the elbow is about 20° lower. These come from different definitions of the segments (Plug-in-Gait’s pelvis from the ASIS and PSIS markers, Xsens’ elbow from its own skeleton); the correlations and the RMS after the offset are the comparable part.

  • Quiet-standing hip flexion of 9° (median over the case study’s trials; up to 19°) is partly where the pelvis markers sit on the body and partly real posture; OpenSim gives 12°.

  • Knee moment in the case study is lower than OpenSim’s (95th percentile 52–56 vs 75–76 N m): the two models put the knee’s centre of rotation differently.

  • Muscle forces are an estimate: MyoFullBody’s back extensors cannot supply the low-back moment of heavy lifting in about 30 % of the frames, which then need reserve torques (Muscle forces).

  • Raw-take defects of the case study (a wrong skeleton template, rotated head labels, labelling gaps) are listed on The case study: box lifting with OptiTrack.
