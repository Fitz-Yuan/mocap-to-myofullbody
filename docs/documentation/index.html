    Mocap to MSK · MMH Elevator case study · updated 15 Oct 2026

    Mocap to MSK Documentation

    Website home · Motion viewer · Source on GitHub

    How motion-capture marker trajectories become motions and joint loads of a MuJoCo musculoskeletal model (MyoFullBody by default, or one of seven others), worked through on the elevator lifting study and checked on two public datasets: joint angles, joint loads, and for the case study the box and the scene. This page covers each step, the scripts, how every step was checked, what the checks found, and what is still open. Muscle activations and forces are not produced here: they need a muscle-level method on top of these motions, such as static optimisation or a controller trained to track them.

    Summary: accuracy, validation and status

    • The pipeline runs from the raw OptiTrack takes to motion clips with no Motive, no OpenSim and no file processed by hand. One command, CPU only, about an hour for the 22 trials of the case study.
    • Before the calibration fix, against an earlier version built from a collaborator's export of the same takes, it fitted the markers equally well (median residual 9.2 vs 9.5 mm) and kept 73 vs 76 carry cycles. The final run (static-trial calibration) keeps 63 of 110 carry cycles (25.9 min) after the per-cycle checks and a manual review.
    • Main accuracy problem found and traced: joint angles carried per-subject offsets (quiet-standing knee flexion 10–24° in 16 of 22 trials) that the marker residual cannot show. The cause is the subject calibration: a marker's position on its bone and the joint angle are not separable from the motion trials alone. Calibrating on each subject's static standing trial removes it: quiet-standing knee flexion median 18° → 0.1°, knee offset against Xsens from −8 / −10° to +1.3 / −2.4°. It is the default of run_all.sh (M2M_STATIC=1).
    • Against OpenSim (Scale + Marker Placer + IK, Rajagopal 2016, same markers): marker residual median 9.2 vs 19.8 mm, quiet-standing knee 0.1° vs 10.6°.
    • Any model, any marker set: eight musculoskeletal models (MyoFullBody, MS-Human-700 and its legs, MASS, Rajagopal 2016, Lai–Uhlrich 2022, Gait2354, MyoLeg), Plug-in-Gait or the ISB set, C3D and TRC input with force plates. On public Vicon data the knee flexion follows Vicon's own Plug-in-Gait output with r = 0.996 (36 walking trials).
    • Inverse dynamics (joint loads): low-back L5/S1 extension moment agrees with OpenSim Inverse Dynamics given the same loads (r = 0.984, RMS 13 N m); the predicted total ground force matches the lab's force plates to 1.8 % of body weight, and the plates give back the box mass (13.2 vs 13.6 kg).

    Processing stages: parsing, marker conditioning, IK, reference, QA

    1

    Stage 1 · Reading the recording: .tak (OLE compound file), C3D and TRC, force plates

    mocap2myo/tak.py · pipeline/read_raw.py · pipeline/validate/tak_reader.py

    A .tak file is a Microsoft OLE compound file, which the open-source library olefile can open. The marker trajectories are in Channels.dat: one record of frame number plus x, y, z per marker per frame, with no record when the marker was not seen. Marker names, the skeleton or rigid body each marker belongs to, and each rigid body's marker layout are in Nodes.dat. We worked out this layout ourselves; there is no official specification and no existing open-source reader.

    In .takOut tak_cache/: marker trajectories per trial (Motive world, Y up, metres)
    Checked. All 11 processed takes in the dataset (S01, S03, S04, S09, S10, S11: anatomy, platform and S03's two NE trials) are identical to Motive's own TRC exports, largest difference 0.005 mm (float32 rounding). Raw takes match the TRC exports wherever both have a sample; they differ only where the lab edited the take in Motive before exporting.
    Caution. Verified for this study's Motive version. For data from another Motive version, compare against one TRC export first: python -m mocap2myo.tak IN.tak --compare EXPORT.trc.

    Other labs' data comes as C3D or TRC (mocap2myo/readers.py; C3D through ezc3d), described by a dataset profile (datasets/*.toml: file kind, which files are trials and static trials and how they are named, up axis, units, rate, where heights and masses come from, the task). The reader puts the markers in metres in a Z-up world, keeps the lab software's model outputs (joint angles, forces, segment points) apart from the markers, and turns each force plate into the ground reaction on the body per marker frame (the analog samples of a frame averaged): force, centre of pressure on the plate surface (also on inclined plates), free moment about the vertical, plate corners, in the markers' world frame. These go into the marker files for the later stages, with the other analog channels (EMG) by name. Every stage keeps the recording's frame rate.

    Checked on public data. Vielemeyer et al. 2026 (Vicon, Plug-in-Gait, 200 Hz, three AMTI plates, subject Ref01): the 39 markers found by name, 76 segment points and 82 model outputs kept apart; body mass from the plates 67.0 kg (12 level trials, mean vertical force between two plate contacts corrected for the pelvis's vertical acceleration, range 66.8–67.2; the static trial 67.1 kg; the article lists 65 kg), the same on the inclined plates of the ramp trials; centre of pressure inside the plate and under the stance foot (from the heel marker to just past the toe marker) in every loaded frame. IK on one level trial (1.65 s): body-marker residual median 7.0 mm, 95th percentile 23 mm; static pose knees 0°, hips −2° (without the static trial: hips −11°, ankles 7–9°). Moissenet and Schreiber 2019 (Qualisys, 100 Hz, one subject, 25 trials): 52 markers, height and mass from the C3D, mass from single support on the plates 67.1 kg (C3D: 67.0). The TRC route reproduces the take route on a Motive TRC export (static trial, largest difference 0.005 mm).

    Stage 2 · Marker conditioning: label mapping, mislabel rejection, rigid-body fits

    pipeline/prepare_markers.py · mocap2myo/markerset.py · tasks/box_lift/box_fit.py (box, shelf, platform, floor)

    Brings every trial to one format: the 39 Plug-in-Gait body markers, the box's 6 markers and pose, the shelf and elevator platform positions, and the floor height.

    • Coordinates are turned to Z up.
    • Mislabelled samples are dropped: a marker's distance to the steadiest markers on its own segment should stay nearly constant, and a sample that breaks this is treated as a wrong label.
    • Missing pelvis markers are refilled from the other three, using the pelvis as a rigid body.
    • Box: a template of its 6 markers is taken while the box rests in S03; each frame's box position and orientation come from a rigid (Kabsch) fit of that template.
    • Shelf and floor: static markers, sorted by height.
    • S01's labels are renamed (below), and the four head markers of every trial are assigned by their front/back and left/right position.

    Stage 3 · Subject marker registration and per-frame bounded least-squares IK

    pipeline/solve_ik.py · mocap2myo/ik.py (the solver)

    IK finds the joint angles that bring the model's virtual markers as close as possible to the measured ones. It is the same idea as OpenSim's IK, written directly on MuJoCo's MyoFullBody and solved with scipy.optimize.least_squares; joint limits are the bounds of the variables.

    • Scaling. The model stays as it is; the markers are scaled by the ratio of model height to subject height.
    • Calibration, once per subject. 40 frames with varied poses are taken from all of the subject's trials, plus standing frames, which must have flat feet. The solver alternates between solving IK with the current marker placements and updating where each marker sits on its segment from the solved poses, like OpenSim's Scale plus Marker Placer. A marker may move from its starting position on the segment by up to 6 cm, 10 cm for the foot markers (on thick-soled slides) and 15 cm for the wand, trunk and head markers, whose placement varies most.
    • Static trial (the default). The subject's static standing trial joins the calibration: in its frames the hips, knees, ankles and subtalar joints are pulled toward 0°, and they carry half the weight of every update of the marker placements. They place only the pelvis and leg markers (M2M_STATIC_MARKERS=legs); the arm and trunk markers come from the motion trials. run_all.sh turns this on (M2M_STATIC=1); the default in pipeline/config.py is off, so a stage run on its own needs M2M_STATIC=1. Why it is needed: see joint-angle validation and static-trial calibration.
    • Per-frame solve. 37 degrees of freedom: pelvis 6, legs 14, lumbar 3, arms 14. Other joints follow the model's coupling constraints. Each frame starts from the previous one, and the pelvis starting pose comes from a rigid fit to the four pelvis markers.
    • Safeguards. Markers are low-pass filtered at 6 Hz. A robust loss weakens the pull of a marker that is far off. Feet may not go through the floor, a standing foot stays where it was planted, and joint speed is capped so the solution cannot flip between frames.
    In stage 2 markersOut ik/: joint angles (qpos) per frame, each marker's residual, and the frames the IK could not solve
    Fit (final run). Body-marker residual per trial: median 7.7–14.3 mm, 95th percentile 25–48 mm (the IK's own measure, on the height-scaled, filtered markers). Against the raw markers in the subject's own size, as used for the OpenSim comparison: median 7.9–14.3 mm per trial (9.2 mm, median over trials); OpenSim IK on the same markers 14.6–27.5 mm (19.8 mm; S09: 16.0 and 14.9 mm).
    Why the static trial. From the motion trials alone, the calibration can trade where markers sit on a segment against the pose itself: a knee marker a few centimetres further back fits as well as a knee bent 20°, which shifts the joint angles of every frame of that subject. The static trial removes that freedom; see joint-angle validation and static-trial calibration.
    On the GPU (M2M_IK_DEVICE=gpu, pip install -e ".[gpu]"; mocap2myo/ik_gpu.py). The same per-frame problem (markers, robust loss, toe prior, floor, stance anchors, smoothing, speed and joint limits), with the kinematics and marker Jacobians in Warp kernels (float64) and a projected Levenberg–Marquardt step per frame. The frame-to-frame recursion runs in parallel over time: the trial is cut into 64-frame chunks that each step through their own frames, and chunks whose start differs from where the previous chunk ended are run again until every chunk starts where the one before it ended (two passes on every trial tried). Subject calibration stays on the CPU. On S03, S08 and S10 (11,124–13,049 frames): 37–108 s per trial on a shared GPU against 20–30 min per trial on one CPU core; residual median and 95th percentile equal to the CPU's to 0.01 mm; joint angles within 0.003–0.14° RMS (a few frames of S10, where the CPU's own arm fit is poor, differ by up to 7.8°). For a whole dataset the trials still run one after another (about 20–25 min for the 22 trials, against about 45 min on 22 cores); batching all trials into one call is the next step. Only MyoFullBody with Plug-in-Gait at 100 Hz for now; other profiles, marker sets and rates stop with a message.
    Model limits. The knee has no axial rotation, so all twisting during turns goes to the hip. Deep squats go past the model's 120° joint limits. There is no neck joint, so head markers carry a low weight (an optional neck for the IK, M2M_NECK=1, lets the head follow its markers; it is not validated).

    Stage 4 · Reference construction: floor alignment, box pose, grasp and support labels

    tasks/box_lift/build_reference.py
    • The body is aligned with the floor (the elevator platform). Subjects wore thick-soled slides; with the static-trial calibration the barefoot model's soles sit between 1.3 cm below and 2.6 cm above the platform before this shift (median over trials −0.2 cm).
    • Box size: width and height from the marker layout; length from the distance between the palms while carrying (0.428 m). Box mass 30 lb (13.6 kg).
    • Shelf top height = height of the box bottom while the box rests on the shelf.
    • Grasp label: the box is off the floor and shelf, and that palm is close enough to the box surface.
    • Support state (floor, hands, shelf) is labelled, and each carry cycle is found: box lifted off the floor until it is set back down.

    Stage 5 · QA gating and carry-cycle segmentation

    pipeline/qa.py · tasks/box_lift/build_dataset.py · pipeline/report.py
    • Each carry cycle becomes one clip at 100 Hz.
    • Clips that fail the checks are dropped automatically: 95th-percentile marker residual above 6 cm, unsolved frames, sudden root jumps, pelvis markers far off, or the pelvis turning too fast (IK loses the pelvis when a deep squat goes past the model's limits).
    • A manual review on top: after viewing the reference motions, subjects S10 and S11 and the 5th cycle of S09 Spring were removed.
    • report.py grades every trial on gaps, fit, leave-one-marker-out prediction, joint flips and standing posture (PASS / WARN / FAIL with reasons). It does not drop anything by itself.

    Models and marker sets: model profiles, anatomical landmarks, marker placement

    The model and the marker set are both data, so the stages run with any pair of them. A model profile (mocap2myo/profiles/<name>.toml, chosen with M2M_MODEL) names the model file and how to fetch it, its root and neutral pose, the joints the IK solves, which bodies are the canonical segments (pelvis, torso, head, femur, tibia, foot, humerus, ...), the inverse-dynamics joints with the axes their moments are reported about, and the feet and hands. mocap2myo/landmarks.py computes some 70 anatomical landmarks from the model's own bones: joint centres and bony points such as the ASIS, the femoral epicondyles, C7 and the styloids.

    A marker set (mocap2myo/markersets/<name>.toml, chosen with M2M_MARKER_SET or the dataset profile's marker_set) describes each marker against the anatomy, not a model, so one file serves every model: the segment it moves with, the landmark under it, its offset from that landmark in the segment's anatomical axes (anterior, superior, lateral, mm), its weight and its calibration group (loose: may move up to 15 cm, foot, or the default 6 cm). Roles name the head, pelvis, heel, toe and ankle markers; alias sets map other labellings onto it and belong to their set.

    • A marker on a segment or landmark the model lacks, or on a body no IK joint moves relative to the pelvis (MyoLeg's trunk, the arms of MS-Human-700 legs), is left out.
    • A joint with no active marker beyond it (the neck of MASS and MS-Human-700 with the ISB set, which has no head markers) is held at the neutral pose instead of solved; the toes keep their prior.
    • On MyoFullBody, Plug-in-Gait keeps the hand-tuned starting placements the case study was calibrated with (the set's [override]), so the case-study results are unchanged; on any other model, and for any other set, the placements come from the landmarks.
    • The frame-to-frame smoothing of the IK is scaled with the square root of the frame rate (its lag in frames grows with the weight squared), so a 200 Hz recording is smoothed over the same time as a 100 Hz one.
    Checked. All profiles run end to end on one ramp trial (200 Hz) and one Moissenet trial (100 Hz); see public-data validation.

MyoFullBody's and MyoLeg's knee rotates about the tibia's origin, 8.9 mm from the joint frame of the OpenSim knee it was converted from (up to 15 mm at the ankle in deep flexion). The opt-in profiles myofullbody_kneeframe and myoleg_kneeframe move it there. Against Xsens (S11 Spring) the knee offset moves by about 1° (right +1.3 → +0.4°, left −2.4 → −3.2°) and the RMS after the offset is unchanged (3.8 → 3.9°, 3.0 → 3.0°), so the default stays.

    Adding a model.     The lifting task (tasks/box_lift/: box loads, hand contact, the reference) is written for MyoFullBody's bodies; other models run with M2M_TASK=none. Model files other than MyoFullBody are fetched at pinned commits into M2M_MODELS_DIR and keep their own licences (mocap2myo/profiles/sources.toml).

    Joint-angle validation: errors the marker residual cannot show

    The marker residual only says how tightly the model was fitted to the markers it saw. Three checks look further.

    Leave-one-marker-out cross-validation: marker prediction error

    Every body marker is left out in turn (one frame per second, all 22 trials), the frame is solved again, and the model's prediction of the missing marker is compared with where it was measured. Script: pipeline/validate/lomo.py. Final run (static-trial calibration); per marker, the median over trials:

    • Prediction error per trial: median 12.3–24.0 mm (median 14.45), against a residual of 6.6–11.3 mm with all markers.
    • Legs are pinned down well: ankle 8–10 mm, knee 11–13 mm, shank 7–9 mm.
    • Shoulder 23–25 mm (residual 17 mm) and elbow about 19 mm (residual 12 mm): the upper arm is the loosest part of the fit.
    • Finger 37–44 mm and toe 19–21 mm: each is the only marker that sets the wrist or toe angle, so leaving it out is expected to fail.
    • This test cannot see a whole-subject offset: if the calibration bent the knees, the other markers still predict each other well.

    Quiet-standing bias in knee and hip flexion

    Quiet standing frames are found from the markers alone: pelvis and both ankles moving less than 5 cm/s for 0.5 s, pelvis within 3 cm of its upright height. A person standing quietly has the knee at about 0–5° and the hip near 0°. Before the calibration fix (motion trials only):

    • Knee flexion in quiet standing: 0–24°. At least 10° in 16 of 22 trials, above 20° in 7 (S07, S08, S09, S10 Spring).
    • Hip flexion in quiet standing: up to 28° from neutral. Above 10° in 15 trials, above 20° in 5 (S01, S02 Solid, S10).
    • Asymmetry within a subject, e.g. S07: right knee about 0°, left 20° in the most extended frames.

    Final run (static-trial calibration): knee flexion in quiet standing median 0.1°, at least 10° in no trial; |hip flexion| median 9.4°, above 10° in 10 trials (see the QA report below).

    Reading. Neither the residual nor the leave-one-marker-out test catches this, and Xsens independently points the same way at the knee. The static standing trials settle the cause: with the motion-trial calibration, the IK puts subjects who stand still and upright at 13–26° of knee flexion or 14–21° of hip extension, while their bone-length ratios match the model within about 5 % (S02 and S08 have nearly the same ratios and 0° vs 24° of knee flexion). So it is not a segment-length problem. The calibration lets each marker move a few centimetres on its bone, and a knee marker a few centimetres further back fits as well as a knee bent 20°: from the motion trials alone the two cannot be told apart.

    Static-trial calibration: separating marker placement from joint angle

    Each subject was recorded standing still and upright for 5 s before the task (raw_tak/static/S<ss>_Anatomy.tak; S07 has none and uses quiet standing from its trials). Those frames join the calibration with the hips, knees, ankles and subtalar joints pulled toward 0°, and carry half the weight of every update of the marker placements (mocap2myo.ik.calibrate(static=...)). This is the role of the static trial in OpenSim's Scale and Marker Placer. It is switched on by M2M_STATIC=1, which run_all.sh sets; the default in pipeline/config.py is 0, so a stage run on its own (for example pipeline/solve_ik.py) needs M2M_STATIC=1 to reproduce these results.

    Inverse dynamics: subtree Newton–Euler joint moments, ground reaction and L5/S1 compression

    Net joint forces and moments come from Newton–Euler sums over subtrees of MyoFullBody, scaled to the subject's size and body mass (plus the 1–3 kg COSMED K5 system the subjects wore in these trials, lumped at the torso). For a joint, the parent segment's force and moment on everything distal to it equal the rate of change of that subtree's momentum minus gravity and the external loads on it (mocap2myo/kinetics.py, pipeline/kinetics.py). Body positions are low-passed at 6 Hz before the double differentiation (M2M_KIN_LOWPASS_HZ), as the markers are for the IK. The floor height comes from the standing foot (a running low percentile of the sole points that are still), so a foot flat on the ground counts as in contact. Body mass comes from the static trial's force plates when they weigh within 80–125 % of the listed mass. Frames the IK did not solve (listed in its output) are interpolated and shaded like the undetermined ones.

    Using your own data: requirements, folder layout and dataset profiles

    A dataset needs labelled markers, a known marker set and one static standing trial per subject. Its settings (file kind and naming, units, subjects, marker set, task) go into a small profile, so nothing in the code changes.

    1. Labelled marker trajectories: OptiTrack .tak takes, C3D or TRC files. The labels the recording software assigned are enough; unlabelled point clouds are not. Force plates (C3D) and any frame rate are taken as they are.
    2. A known marker set: Plug-in-Gait and the 52-marker ISB / Dumas set ship with the code; another set is one TOML file in mocap2myo/markersets/ (see models and marker sets).
    3. Enough markers per segment to fix its orientation (ideally three).
    4. A static standing trial per subject, to anchor the joint angles (static-trial calibration).
    5. Subject height (for scaling) and mass (for the joint loads): a CSV, or the C3D parameters.

    Tasks. Everything about the lifting task (box template and pose, shelf, platform and floor, carry cycles, the reference, the dataset, the box's load on the hands and the viewer scene) is in tasks/box_lift/; another task is another package in tasks/ with the same hooks (tasks/box_lift/__init__.py), chosen with M2M_TASK. With M2M_TASK=none the pipeline stops at the body motion: IK, QA, report and joint loads with the ground as the only external load; the viewer then reads the IK output (whole trials).

    Limitations to keep in mind.

    • Models are scaled uniformly by height, not per segment.
    • Skin markers move on the soft tissue by 1–3 cm in large motions; residuals of about 1 cm are close to that floor.
    • MyoFullBody has no knee axial rotation and no neck joint (the IK can add one, M2M_NECK=1, not validated); deep squats reach its 120° joint limits.
    • The lifting task is written for MyoFullBody's bodies; other models run the case study with M2M_TASK=none.

    OpenSim comparison: the files it needs

    pipeline/validate/opensim_ik.py needs three files in opensim_setup/ (M2M_OSIM_SETUP_DIR), not part of the repository, and opensim from conda (conda install -c opensim-org opensim; OSIM_PY if it is another Python):

    • Model.osim: the Rajagopal et al. (2016) full-body model (SimTK).
    • markers.xml: an OpenSim MarkerSet for that model with the labels of the marker set (Plug-in-Gait: LASI, RKNE, C7, ...).
    • A ScaleTool setup (M2M_OSIM_SCALE_SETUP): its measurement set defines the scaling and its Marker Placer task set the marker weights; markers it switches off are switched on at a low weight. The script sets the model and marker files, the subject's mass and height, and the static trial.

    Viewer files

    python pipeline/export_viewer.py model OUT_DIR once per model, then clip TRIAL OUT_DIR per trial (--clip N: one carry cycle of the task; --public NAME: a de-identified clip; --ghost-ref-dir: a second run overlaid; --help lists the rest). Joint loads and the OpenSim overlay are included when kin/ and opensim_id/ exist. A clip carries body poses per frame, measured and model markers, joint-angle and joint-load curves, force arrows, the QA grades, and two task-neutral lists the viewer draws as given: objects (shape, size, pose) and state tracks (e.g. hand contact). The viewer reads the files in the browser; nothing is uploaded.

    Glossary: markers, labels, IK, calibration, residual

    Marker and label
    A reflective ball on the body. Its label tells IK which point on the model it belongs to; without that, IK cannot tell where to pull.
    Gap
    Frames where a marker was hidden. Short gaps are interpolated, pelvis gaps are refilled as a rigid body, and IK handles the rest.
    Plug-in-Gait
    A common 39-marker full-body placement (LASI, RKNE, C7, ...). Used by the elevator study.
    Kabsch fit
    Finds the rotation and translation that best map a set of points known in an object's frame onto their measured positions. Used for the box and the pelvis.
    Forward kinematics
    From joint angles to the position of every segment and virtual marker. Computed by MuJoCo.
    Least-squares IK
    The reverse: the joint angles that minimise the weighted sum of squared distances between virtual and measured markers.
    Calibration
    Finding where each marker sits on its segment, since placement differs from person to person.
    Residual
    How far a virtual marker still is from the measured one after the fit: a measure of how tight the fit is, not of whether the joint angles are right.
    Model profile
    A small TOML file that tells the pipeline how a model is built: its file, root, neutral pose, IK joints, canonical segments, kinetics joints, feet and hands.
    Marker set
    A TOML file that places each marker on the anatomy: segment, landmark, offset in the segment's anatomical axes, weight. The same file works on every model.
    Leave one marker out
    Solve without one marker and predict where it should be. Tests whether the other markers pin that segment down.

    Code reading order for new contributors

    1. mocap2myo/tak.py: the format notes at the top explain what a raw file contains.
    2. pipeline/prepare_markers.py: read convert() from top to bottom; each helper is one bullet of stage 2 (the box and scene part is tasks/box_lift/box_fit.py).
    3. mocap2myo/markerset.py and mocap2myo/markersets/plug_in_gait.toml (which segment and landmark each marker is on; the model side is mocap2myo/profile.py), then the core, mocap2myo/ik.py: MarkerModel.solve (one frame), calibrate (calibration) and ik_trial (a whole trial, including foot locking).
    4. pipeline/solve_ik.py: runs the functions above in parallel with the current settings (pipeline/config.py).
    5. tasks/box_lift/build_reference.py, pipeline/qa.py, tasks/box_lift/build_dataset.py: scene, checks, clips.
    6. pipeline/report.py, pipeline/validate/lomo.py, pipeline/validate/xsens.py: the validation.
    7. pipeline/run_all.sh: the script that chains it all.

    For background, work through OpenSim's Scale and IK tutorial first; the principle is the same. Solver details: scipy least_squares. Model and forward kinematics: MuJoCo documentation.
