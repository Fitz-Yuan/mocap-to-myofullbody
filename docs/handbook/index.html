mocap-to-myofullbody · MMH Elevator case study · updated 15 Oct 2026

Mocap to MyoFullBody Handbook

Repository home · Motion viewer · Source on GitHub

How the motion-capture marker trajectories of the elevator lifting study become reference motions the MyoFullBody musculoskeletal model can track: joint angles plus the box and the scene. This page covers each step, the scripts, how every step was checked, what the checks found, and what is still open. Muscle activations are not produced here; they come later, when an RL controller is trained to track these references.

Summary: status and key findings

  • The pipeline runs from the raw OptiTrack takes to motion clips with no Motive, no OpenSim and no file processed by hand. One command, CPU only, about an hour for the 22 trials of the case study.
  • Against the earlier version built from a collaborator export, it fits the markers equally well (median residual 9.2 vs 9.5 mm) and keeps 73 vs 76 carry cycles.
  • Main accuracy problem found and traced: joint angles carried per-subject offsets (quiet-standing knee flexion 10–24° in 16 of 22 trials) that the marker residual cannot show. The cause is the subject calibration: a marker's position on its bone and the joint angle are not separable from the motion trials alone. Calibrating on each subject's static standing trial removes it: knee offset against Xsens from −8 / −10° to +0.7 / −2.4°.

Processing stages

1

Stage 1 · .tak parsing (OLE compound file: Channels.dat, Nodes.dat)

optitrack_tak.py · elev_tak_cache.py · tak_reader_check.py

A .tak file is a Microsoft OLE compound file, which the open-source library olefile can open. The marker trajectories are in Channels.dat: one record of frame number plus x, y, z per marker per frame, with no record when the marker was not seen. Marker names, the skeleton or rigid body each marker belongs to, and each rigid body's marker layout are in Nodes.dat. We worked out this layout ourselves; there is no official specification and no existing open-source reader.

In .takOut tak_cache/: marker trajectories per trial (Motive world, Y up, metres)
Checked. All 11 processed takes in the dataset (S01, S03, S04, S09, S10, S11: anatomy, platform and S03's two NE trials) are identical to Motive's own TRC exports, largest difference 0.005 mm (float32 rounding). Raw takes match the TRC exports wherever both have a sample; they differ only where the lab edited the take in Motive before exporting.
Caution. Verified for this study's Motive version. For data from another Motive version, compare against one TRC export first: optitrack_tak.py IN.tak --compare EXPORT.trc.
2

Stage 2 · Marker conditioning: label mapping, mislabel rejection, rigid-body fits

elev_convert.py

Brings every trial to one format: the 39 Plug-in-Gait body markers, the box's 6 markers and pose, the shelf and elevator platform positions, and the floor height.

  • Coordinates are turned to Z up.
  • Mislabelled samples are dropped: a marker's distance to the steadiest markers on its own segment should stay nearly constant, and a sample that breaks this is treated as a wrong label.
  • Missing pelvis markers are refilled from the other three, using the pelvis as a rigid body.
  • Box: a template of its 6 markers is taken while the box rests in S03; each frame's box position and orientation come from a rigid (Kabsch) fit of that template.
  • Shelf and floor: static markers, sorted by height.
  • Raw-take route only: S01's labels are renamed (below), and the four head markers of every trial are assigned by their front/back and left/right position.
In step 1 trajectories, subject height and weight (meta/Anthropometrics.xlsx, the lab's own file)Out npz/ or npz_tak/
Checked. Both routes compared point by point. In 18 of 22 trials, 98–99.9% of the shared samples are identical; box pose, floor and shelf heights are identical in all 22. S06: the export was smoothed (3–5 mm per point, no time shift). S01: it was reconstructed again in Motive (differences below 1 mm). The default route was re-run on S09 and its output is unchanged.
3

Stage 3 · Subject marker registration and per-frame bounded least-squares IK

elev_ik.py · mmh_ik.py (the solver)

IK finds the joint angles that bring the model's virtual markers as close as possible to the measured ones. It is the same idea as OpenSim's IK, written directly on MuJoCo's MyoFullBody and solved with scipy.optimize.least_squares; joint limits are the bounds of the variables.

  • Scaling. The model stays as it is; the markers are scaled by the ratio of model height to subject height.
  • Calibration, once per subject. 40 frames with varied poses are taken from all of the subject's trials. The solver alternates between solving IK with the current marker placements and updating where each marker sits on its segment from the solved poses, like OpenSim's Scale plus Marker Placer. Standing frames must have flat feet. A marker may move up to about 6 cm from its starting position on the segment.
  • Per-frame solve. 37 degrees of freedom: pelvis 6, legs 14, lumbar 3, arms 14. Other joints follow the model's coupling constraints. Each frame starts from the previous one, and the pelvis starting pose comes from a rigid fit to the four pelvis markers.
  • Safeguards. Markers are low-pass filtered at 6 Hz. A robust loss weakens the pull of a marker that is far off. Feet may not go through the floor, a standing foot stays where it was planted, and joint speed is capped so the solution cannot flip between frames.
In step 2 npzOut ik/: joint angles (qpos) per frame and each marker's residual
Fit. Body-marker residual per trial: median 7.5–12.3 mm, 95th percentile 25–49 mm (raw-take route; v10 is the same). OpenSim IK on S09 with the same marker data: median about 21 mm.
Problem. The calibration can trade where markers sit on a segment against the pose itself. With segment lengths only scaled by height, a mismatch in bone lengths is absorbed partly by moving markers and partly by bending joints, which shifts the joint angles of every frame of that subject. See "Validation".
Model limits. The knee has no axial rotation, so all twisting during turns goes to the hip. Deep squats go past the model's 120° joint limits. There is no neck joint, so head markers carry a low weight.
4

Stage 4 · Reference construction: floor alignment, box pose, grasp and support labels

elev_build_reference.py
  • The body is lowered onto the floor. Subjects wore thick-soled slides, so the barefoot model floats 2.5–5.5 cm.
  • Box size: width and height from the marker layout; length from the distance between the palms while carrying (0.426 m). Box mass 30 lb (13.6 kg).
  • Shelf top height = height of the box bottom while the box rests on the shelf.
  • Grasp label: the box is off the floor and shelf, and that palm is close enough to the box surface.
  • Support state (floor, hands, shelf) is labelled, and each carry cycle is found: box lifted off the floor until it is set back down.
Out ref/ or ref_tak/
5

Stage 5 · QA gating and carry-cycle segmentation

elev_qa.py · elev_build_dataset.py · merge_lift_stage3.py · elev_report.py
  • Each carry cycle becomes one clip at 100 Hz.
  • Clips that fail the checks are dropped automatically: 95th-percentile marker residual above 6 cm, unsolved frames, sudden pelvis jumps, or the pelvis turning too fast (IK loses the pelvis when a deep squat goes past the model's limits).
  • Human review on top: after watching the replay videos, subjects S10 and S11 and the 5th cycle of S09 Spring were removed.
  • The clips are merged with the public MMH lifting data, and starting muscle activations are computed for each clip.
  • New: elev_report.py grades every trial on gaps, fit, leave-one-marker-out prediction, joint flips and standing posture (PASS / WARN / FAIL with reasons). It does not drop anything by itself.
Out v10: 76 cycles, 31.1 min. Raw-take route: 73 cycles, 30.0 min (S01–S09)

Validation of joint-angle accuracy

The marker residual only says how tightly the model was fitted to the markers it saw. Three checks look further.

Leave-one-marker-out (LOMO) cross-validation

Every body marker is left out in turn (one frame per second, all 22 trials), the frame is solved again, and the model's prediction of the missing marker is compared with where it was measured. Script: elev_ik_lomo.py.

  • Prediction error per trial: median 11.8–20.2 mm (median 14.9), against a residual of 6.6–10.4 mm with all markers.
  • Legs are pinned down well: ankle 7–8 mm, knee 9–11 mm, shank 7–13 mm.
  • Shoulder and elbow about 22 mm (residual 14–17 mm): the upper arm is the loosest part of the fit.
  • Finger 32–36 mm and toe 26 mm: each is the only marker that sets the wrist or toe angle, so leaving it out is expected to fail.
  • This test cannot see a whole-subject offset: if the calibration bent the knees, the other markers still predict each other well.

Xsens MVN knee-angle agreement (S11 Spring)

The subjects also wore an Xsens suit. Only S11 Spring NE has a usable export (a c3d of MVN's 64 virtual anatomical points, 60 Hz; the other trials exist only as raw .mvn files, and S11 Solid's c3d is empty). The same geometric angles were computed on both sides and aligned by cross-correlation (lag 0.05 s). Script: elev_xsens_compare.py.

AngleCorrelationMean offset (IK − Xsens)RMS after removing the offset
Right knee0.994−8.2°3.2°
Left knee0.995−10.0°2.8°

Negative offset = the IK knee is more flexed (the included angle is smaller). The knee's shape over time matches almost exactly; the level is 8–10° apart, and the model's own geometry accounts for only 1.4° of it. Elbow and trunk were also computed but are not comparable: Xsens gives the acromion rather than the shoulder joint centre, and the model's elbow carries a 13° geometric offset. Xsens has its own errors of several degrees, so this is supporting evidence, not ground truth.

Quiet-standing posture bias

Quiet standing frames are found from the markers alone: pelvis and both ankles moving less than 5 cm/s for 0.5 s, pelvis within 3 cm of its upright height. A person standing quietly has the knee at about 0–5° and the hip near 0°.

  • Knee flexion in quiet standing: 0–24°. At least 10° in 16 of 22 trials, above 20° in 7 (S07, S08, S09, S10 Spring).
  • Hip flexion in quiet standing: up to 28° from neutral. Above 10° in 15 trials, above 20° in 5 (S01, S02 Solid, S10).
  • Asymmetry within a subject, e.g. S07: right knee about 0°, left 20° in the most extended frames.
Reading. Neither the residual nor the leave-one-marker-out test catches this, and Xsens independently points the same way at the knee. The static standing trials settle the cause: with the same calibration, the IK puts subjects who stand still and upright at 13–26° of knee flexion or 14–21° of hip extension, while their bone-length ratios match the model within about 5 % (S02 and S08 have nearly the same ratios and 0° vs 24° of knee flexion). So it is not a segment-length problem. The calibration lets each marker move a few centimetres on its bone, and a knee marker a few centimetres further back fits as well as a knee bent 20°: from the motion trials alone the two cannot be told apart.

Calibration fix: anchoring on the static trial

Each subject was recorded standing still and upright for 5 s before the task (Subject<ss>_Anatomy.tak; S07 has none and uses quiet standing from its trials). Those frames join the calibration with the hips, knees, ankles and subtalar joints pulled toward 0°, and carry half the weight of every update of the marker placements (ELEV_STATIC=1; mmh_ik.calibrate(static=...)). This is the role of the static trial in OpenSim's Scale and Marker Placer.

Data anomalies in the raw takes

  • S01 was labelled with the wrong skeleton template. Motive used a template with ISB landmark names (LIAS, LFAL, LHLE, ...) on the subject's Plug-in-Gait markers. An anatomy-based name map turns them back (ISB_TO_PIG in elev_convert.py); the single elbow marker took either epicondyle name and is merged. The collaborators' export had renamed them the same way, which confirms the map.
  • Head labels rotated by 90° in S01 and S10. The four head markers are now assigned by position in every trial.
  • S01 has no forearm markers (LFRM, RFRM) in any source.
  • Large live-labelling gaps in some markers: C7 in S02 (75–98% of frames missing) and S11 Solid (88%), RUPA in S06 (65–72%), RTHI in S08 (34–42%). The collaborators' export had these filled.
  • S01's right PSIS is not rigid with the rest of the pelvis in either source (distance to the left PSIS varies by 35–42 mm; 2.5 mm in S03).
  • What the manual Motive processing adds to a raw take is gap filling: in S03, the 5,765 samples the processed take has and the raw labels lack are in none of the unlabelled tracks. A few wrong labels were also removed (8 ASIS frames about 40 mm off).
  • S07 has no anatomy (static) trial in the dataset.

Compute cost and manual effort

Measured machine time for the 22 trials: reading the takes about 1 min, cleaning about 2 min, calibration plus IK about 50 min on 22 CPU cores, references, QA and clips about 1 min. Human work left: one marker-set configuration per new dataset, one reader check against a TRC export, and reviewing the report and replay videos.

For comparison, a manual Motive plus OpenSim workflow is estimated (not measured) at roughly 15–40 person-hours for the same 22 trials: labelling and gap filling per trial, OpenSim scaling per subject, IK setup per trial. Ask the people who do it for real numbers. The automation relies on Motive's live labels; recordings without them (for example the firefighter dynamic trials) need labelling or another source such as Xsens.

CLI usage

# install (Python 3.10+)
pip install -e ".[tak,xsens]"

# whole pipeline from the raw takes (CPU, ~1 h for 22 trials on 22 cores)
ELEV_ROOT=~/data/mmh_elevator IK_WORKERS=22 bash pipeline/run_all.sh

# check the take reader against Motive's TRC exports (folder of .tak / .trc pairs)
python pipeline/tak_reader_check.py DIR

# validation: leave-one-marker-out, Xsens, static-trial diagnosis
ELEV_NPZ_DIR=$ELEV_ROOT/npz_tak ELEV_IK_DIR=$ELEV_ROOT/ik_tak python pipeline/elev_ik_lomo.py
python pipeline/elev_xsens_compare.py $ELEV_ROOT/ik_tak
python pipeline/elev_static_diag.py

# viewer files: the model once, then clips (optionally with a ghost from another run)
python pipeline/elev_export_viewer.py model OUT_DIR
python pipeline/elev_export_viewer.py clip S08_Solid_NE OUT_DIR --clip 1 --ghost-ref-dir OTHER_REF_DIR

# OpenSim baseline (opensim from conda)
python pipeline/elev_osim_ik.py

Data layout under $ELEV_ROOT: raw_tak/ (trial takes), raw_tak/static/ (static trials), meta/anthropometrics.csv; written by the pipeline: tak_cache/, npz_tak/, ik_tak/ (with qa.json, lomo.json, report.json), ref_tak/. No subject data is part of this repository.

Glossary

Marker and label
A reflective ball on the body. Its label tells IK which point on the model it belongs to; without that, IK cannot tell where to pull.
Gap
Frames where a marker was hidden. Short gaps are interpolated, pelvis gaps are refilled as a rigid body, and IK handles the rest.
Plug-in-Gait
A common 39-marker full-body placement (LASI, RKNE, C7, ...). Used by both the elevator study and the public MMH data.
Kabsch fit
Finds the rotation and translation that best map a set of points known in an object's frame onto their measured positions. Used for the box and the pelvis.
Forward kinematics
From joint angles to the position of every segment and virtual marker. Computed by MuJoCo.
Least-squares IK
The reverse: the joint angles that minimise the weighted sum of squared distances between virtual and measured markers.
Calibration
Finding where each marker sits on its segment, since placement differs from person to person.
Residual
How far a virtual marker still is from the measured one after the fit: a measure of how tight the fit is, not of whether the joint angles are right.
Leave one marker out
Solve without one marker and predict where it should be. Tests whether the other markers pin that segment down.

Code reading order

  1. optitrack_tak.py: the format notes at the top explain what a raw file contains.
  2. elev_convert.py: read convert() from top to bottom; each helper is one bullet of step 2.
  3. mmh_ik.py, the core: MARKERS (which segment each marker is on), then MarkerModel.solve (one frame), calibrate (calibration) and ik_trial (a whole trial, including foot locking).
  4. elev_ik.py: runs the functions above in parallel with the current settings.
  5. elev_build_reference.py, elev_qa.py, elev_build_dataset.py: scene, checks, clips.
  6. elev_report.py, elev_ik_lomo.py, elev_xsens_compare.py: the validation.
  7. run_all.sh: the script that chains it all.

For background, work through OpenSim's Scale and IK tutorial first; the principle is the same. Solver details: scipy least_squares. Model and forward kinematics: MuJoCo documentation.
